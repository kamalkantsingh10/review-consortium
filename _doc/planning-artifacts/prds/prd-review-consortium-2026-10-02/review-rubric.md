# PRD Quality Review — Review Consortium

## Overall verdict
This is a substantive, well-grounded PRD. The thesis is clear ("speed and scale", made defensible by mapping every feature to a human-panel safeguard), the research in addendum A1 actually drives features, and the scope and non-goals are honest. Its length (~3,600 words) is within the 5–8 page target. The main risk is in its methods mechanics, which are the part peer reviewers will probe. The PRD contradicts itself about whether a Panel and a Protocol belong to one Study or carry across many studies, and that question is load-bearing for the thesis. The Protocol lock hashes only `protocol.md`, so it does not freeze what reviewers care about most. Several FRs that downstream stories will depend on have no testable consequence.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Real decisions are stated as decisions, with their reasons. §4.3 gives the reason for choosing Qwen3-Omni over "newer cloud-only Qwen versions" ("its open weights stay available"). §6 and FR-5 make a firm call: "No Persona photo… is ever given to a rating Model". FR-8 and Q7 say cross-Model replication is "decided in its Protocol, not by the tool". The Risks table (§9) answers likely objections directly instead of avoiding them, for example the Gemini non-determinism row.

The weaknesses are in how open items are presented. Four of the eight Open Questions answer themselves within the same line: Q1 "This affects Study design, not the tool", Q2 "Built-ins either way", Q4 "Study-level", Q7 "the tool supports it either way". These are useful context for Kamal's meeting with Dr. Alaa, but they are not open questions for the product. Meanwhile, the tensions that actually block design (see Strategic coherence and Done-ness) have no Open Question and no `[NOTE FOR PM]`. The PRD's only `[NOTE FOR PM]` (§7.2, Persona photos) marks a safe, low-stakes deferral.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Open Questions mostly don't block the tool, while the real blockers aren't listed (§10) — Q1, Q2, Q4 and Q7 are Study-design questions that the PRD has already answered for the tool. Panel scope across Studies, context within a Session, and what the lock covers are all undecided but absent from §10. *Fix:* move the Study-design questions to a "For the OLAF Study" sub-list. Add the actual tool-level questions, each with a `[NOTE FOR PM]` at the place in the text where it applies.
- **[medium]** The reproducibility trade-off of using Qwen through a cloud API is understated (§4.3, §9 "Cloud Models drift" row) — the text claims "Runs stay reproducible by self-hosting", but v1 calls Qwen3-Omni through Alibaba Cloud Model Studio. A hosted endpoint's serving stack (quantization, preprocessing, `use_audio_in_video` defaults) may not match self-hosted open weights, so a cloud Run is not guaranteed to reproduce on vLLM. *Fix:* name the trade-off explicitly ("cloud for v1 convenience; self-hosted equivalence not verified"), and either archive the provider's reported model build or add a check that a self-hosted Run is equivalent.

## Substance over theater — strong

There is very little furniture. The JTBD (§2.1) are specific and each one maps to features: same-day turnaround leads to SM-1, "defend the method in peer review" leads to the reporting features, and "cost known before spending" leads to FR-13. The non-users list (§2.2) does real work. There are only two named protagonists. Lena (UJ-3) is the only reason FR-23 and SM-4 exist, so she earns her place. The Vision would not fit another product: "recruited, screened, briefed, blinded, randomized and reported" is a specific claim that features can be checked against. The NFRs are product-specific and each one cites the FR that enforces it (§5). The differentiation claim is backed by A1 ("no open-source VLM panel over video + audio with a rater-flow report was found. Not exhaustive.") and is honestly hedged.

### Findings
- **[low]** Two NFRs are restatements rather than constraints (§5 NFR-1, NFR-4) — they repeat FR-14 and FR-12 word for word. This is harmless, but they add no threshold or new obligation. *Fix:* accept as an index, or fold them into the FRs.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is explicit and the build order follows it (§7.1 starts with board core and the Fake rater, so the pipeline is proven at no cost before spending on Models). The counter-metrics are unusually good: SM-C1 (no tuning after unblinding) and SM-C3 (homogenized raters) show real methodological understanding.

Two things weaken the arc. First, the reuse claim the thesis depends on is internally inconsistent (see the critical finding). Second, the Success Metrics measure only half of the thesis. SM-1 measures speed, but nothing measures defensibility, which the Vision calls the condition for speed being worth anything ("Speed is worthless if a reviewer can dismiss the results").

### Findings
- **[critical]** Panel and Protocol scope contradict each other, between "one Study" and "study after study" (§1 para 2, UJ-1, UJ-2, §3 Panel/Study/Study folder, FR-7, FR-12). The Vision says a researcher "builds and screens a panel once, then runs study after study against it". UJ-1 says "a screened panel he will reuse for every study". But the Glossary defines a Panel as "the set of screened Agents *a Study* rates with… reused across Tests". FR-7 says it "stays valid for every Test *in the Study*". A Study folder holds a single `protocol.md` and Protocol lock. As written, either (a) every new "study" is a new Study folder with no defined way to bring a Panel along, which breaks the thesis, or (b) every new "study" is a new Test inside one Study folder, which means one Protocol lock must cover many research questions. Re-freezing for study #2 would invalidate the lock, and FR-12 doesn't say what happens to earlier main Tests when that happens. UJ-2 ("Kamal runs a new study… freezes the protocol") assumes per-study freezing without saying which folder it happens in. *Fix:* decide the unit. One option: a Panel becomes a first-class artifact that a Study folder can import by hash, and each Study keeps its own Protocol lock. The other: define "Study" as the multi-Test container and make Protocol locks per Test. Then update the Glossary, FR-7, FR-12 and UJ-1/2 to match.
- **[high]** No Success Metric validates the defensibility half of the thesis (§8) — the "Social" JTBD ("Every methods question a reviewer asks has a recorded answer") and the Vision paragraph 3 have no SM. *Fix:* add a primary SM such as "every GUIDE-LLM core item and every Rater-flow step is filled automatically from the Run, with zero manual fields". Optionally add an outcome check: "the OLAF paper's methods section is written from Export + manifest alone".
- **[medium]** SM-2 measures the Model, not the product (§8 SM-2) — whether test–retest ICC reaches 0.75 depends on Gemini/Qwen behaviour, which the tool cannot control. As written it "Validates FR-16", but FR-16 only runs Repeats. *Fix:* make the product metric "ICC and per-Persona variance are computed and reported for every Run". Treat ≥ 0.75 as a Study-level quality threshold, ideally declared in the Protocol.
- **[low]** The "many studies" claim has no throughput or reuse measure (§8) — SM-1 measures one study's turnaround, not that the Panel is actually reused. *Fix:* optional SM: "Panel reused without rebuild across ≥ N Studies/Tests".

## Done-ness clarity — thin

FR-1 to FR-4, FR-10, FR-12, FR-15, FR-17 and FR-21 have crisp, testable consequences ("byte-identical Persona cards", "No request is sent after the ceiling is hit", "Export refuses while any Session is incomplete"). These are excellent. But five FRs have no Consequences block at all, and several of the methods-critical behaviours are left unspecified in exactly the places where a reviewer, or a story writer, would ask "how?".

### Findings
- **[high]** Five FRs have no testable consequence (FR-11, FR-16, FR-18, FR-19, FR-20) — FR-18 Catch trials and FR-19 Rater-flow report are central to the peer-review claim, yet "done" is undefined for both. *Fix:* add at least one consequence to each. Examples: FR-18 "an Agent above the catch-failure threshold is absent from the Export and listed with its reason in the Rater-flow report". FR-19 "counts at each step sum correctly; every exclusion has a reason code". FR-11 "an invalid Test is rejected with file/field/reason; a valid one returns a stable Test ID". FR-16 "Practice clip answers are archived but never exported". FR-20 "status counts match the Archive".
- **[high]** The Protocol lock freezes the prose, not the experiment (FR-12, NFR-4, SM-C1) — only `protocol.md` is hashed. Prompts, Persona cards, Instrument YAML, Test YAML, Model pins, thresholds and seeds can all change after main-Test data exists without breaking the lock. That is exactly the p-hacking SM-C1 says "the Protocol lock exists to stop". *Fix:* define the lock as a hash over `protocol.md` plus `study.yaml`, every Instrument and main-Test YAML, the Persona cards, prompt templates and screening/exclusion thresholds. Add a consequence: "editing any locked input after lock makes `open` refuse for main Tests and is recorded in the manifest."
- **[high]** It is undefined whether context persists within a Session, or how pairwise counterbalancing works (§3 Session, §4.5 description, FR-14, FR-15) — the Glossary says a Session is "one Agent rating a Test's Clips from a fresh context". This implies Clips within a Session share context, which brings carry-over and anchoring effects. The §4.5 description, however, says Clips are presented "in a randomized order with fresh context", which reads as per-Clip. FR-15 has "each pair is presented in both orders". If both orders happen in the same context, the second presentation is contaminated by the first. *Fix:* state the decision. Either use fresh context per Clip/pair (each Session is a sequence of independent calls with the briefing repeated), or keep shared context with randomized order and put both orders of a pair in different Sessions/Repeats. Add a testable consequence.
- **[high]** Perception screening is not required to be construct-relevant (FR-9 vs §9 row 3 and §4.3) — the risk mitigation promises "Perception screening with construct-relevant Clips". However, FR-9's examples ("is the robot moving?", "is there speech?") test low-level perception and would not catch the inverted-"strangeness" failure cited in §4.3. *Fix:* require each main-Test Instrument (or Item/construct) to have at least one screening Clip with a known direction (e.g. a clearly more vs. less "alive" pair). A Model fails for an Instrument if it inverts the direction. Also invalidate screening when Instruments change (FR-7 currently invalidates only on a Model change).
- **[medium]** The thresholds' location and format are unspecified (FR-6, FR-9, FR-18) — FR-6 and FR-9 say "a threshold", and FR-18 says "the threshold set in the Protocol". But the Protocol is `protocol.md` prose, which the tool cannot read reliably. *Fix:* put the thresholds in `study.yaml`/Test YAML (covered by the lock), and have `protocol.md` reference them or be generated from them.
- **[medium]** "Re-executed from the Archive" is undefined given non-deterministic Models (FR-22 consequence vs §9 Gemini row) — re-execution cannot mean identical output for Gemini. It also requires the pinned Model to still exist. *Fix:* define it as "the Archive plus Study folder contain everything needed to re-issue every request byte-identically (prompts, media references, settings)", which is testable with the Fake rater. Leave equality of outputs out of scope.
- **[medium]** Blinding ignores file metadata (FR-10) — "Clip ID reveals nothing about the file name or Condition" covers the ID only. The copied file can still carry container metadata (title, original filename, creation tags) that a VLM may read or that leaks the Condition. *Fix:* add a consequence: "pushed Clips are re-muxed with metadata stripped; a test asserts no source filename or Condition string appears in the stored file."
- **[low]** Behaviour when resuming after hitting the cost ceiling is unspecified (FR-17) — the Run "pauses" at the ceiling, but it doesn't say how it continues (raise the ceiling? confirm?) or whether a partially completed Session is redone. *Fix:* one sentence plus a consequence.

## Scope honesty — adequate

The Non-Goals (§6) do real work: "No statistical analysis beyond the Export" and "No collecting human ratings" are exactly the kind of omissions a reader could otherwise assume are included. Out-of-MVP items are explicit (§7.2), and the partial deferral of FR-20's web page is called out. Every inline `[ASSUMPTION]` is indexed. Open-items density is about 8 Questions, 6 Assumptions and 1 NOTE, which is reasonable for a solo, draft-stage tool.

The gap is that the de-scoping is honest, but a few silent omissions remain where the PRD under-specifies instead of deciding (see Done-ness). The single `[NOTE FOR PM]` is placed at the least contentious deferral.

### Findings
- **[medium]** The GUIDE-LLM coverage claim is larger than the content (FR-22, addendum A1 Reporting standards) — FR-22 says the manifest "covers the GUIDE-LLM items". A1 says there are 12 core items but lists 5, and two of those ("human validation", "code sharing") are not produced by FR-22. *Fix:* list all 12 items in the addendum. Mark which ones the tool fills automatically and which the Study must supply (e.g. human validation, per Non-Goals), and say "auto-fills items X–Y; leaves Z for the author".
- **[low]** Several commands needed by the UJs are missing from the "whole external contract" (§4.4 description) — "Four commands — push, open, status, export — are the whole external contract". But UJ-1 and UJ-2 also need init (FR-1), Persona generation (FR-4), fidelity screening (FR-6), and protocol freezing (FR-12). *Fix:* say "the board's contract", and list the Study/Panel commands separately (names are for architecture to decide).

## Downstream usability — adequate

The Glossary is strong and mostly followed. FR-1 to FR-23, NFR-1 to NFR-8 and SM-1 to SM-5 / SM-C1 to SM-C3 are contiguous and unique. Every feature says which UJs it "Realizes". The Q-references (Q4, Q6, Q7) resolve. Sections mostly stand alone. The weaknesses for architecture and story creation are the unresolved structural questions above (Panel/Study scope, lock contents, context model). These are mechanical to write down, but architecture cannot proceed without deciding them.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Glossary drift on "study" vs "Study" carries the critical ambiguity (§1, UJ-1 "every study", UJ-2 "a new study", SM-1 "a new Study goes from Clips to Export") — lowercase "study" is used for a research iteration and capitalized "Study" for a folder. SM-1's "Study… to Export" mixes units, since Export is per Test. *Fix:* resolve this together with the critical finding, then use the defined term everywhere.
- **[low]** FR-16 contradicts the Glossary on Repeats — the Glossary says a Repeat is "an independent re-run of the same Session", but FR-16 says "Each Session runs the configured number of Repeats" (Repeats inside a Session). *Fix:* "Each Session is run the configured number of Repeats times, each from fresh context."

## Shape fit — strong

The shape is right for a solo-operator, open-source research tool that must survive peer review. It is a capability spec with three lightweight UJs. Two of them are Kamal, and the third (Lena) exists only to justify the open-source distribution. There is no UJ inflation, and the NFRs are operational. The methods-safeguard framing (each feature maps to a human-panel step) suits the stakes. The rigor sits on methods rather than on process ceremony, as the memlog's stakes decision asks.

No findings.
